Absolutely LOVED all of the emails and videos and photos. Thank you so much Amy, Jefferson, Benson, Jackson & Katie, Harrison and Mom. Amanda?? Wilson?? You guys alive?? We'd love to hear from you.

Our Mission was split. As of June 25, had a line drawn the middle of our mission. Well, not quite the middle. We had 205 teaching junior missionaries at the time of the split and we lost about 80 of them to the brand new Philippines Lingayen mission. It was a busy crazy week for President and Sister Foster. We had a busy one too. We did 21 junior missionary apartment inspections. That is alot of missionaries that we got to talk to. We are only in their apartment for 15 minutes but it is so fantastically awesome to be around these hardworking, spiritual, fun, dedicated young missionaries. This week we should get most of the other apartments inspected. Plus we have 2 moves, meaning that we're moving 2 companionships out of their current apartment, taking all of their stuff and all of the mission stuff which includes the fridge, cooktop, washing machine, beds, wardrobes, dishes, etc and delivering to the new apartment. Should be super fun.

Let us tell you about last Tuesday - it's just a glimpse into what almost everyday is like here in the Philippines Urdaneta Mission - the greatest place on earth to serve a mission!

You can’t make this stuff up - you really can’t. It’s Tuesday. It was supposed to be a normal easy day. We had 4 inspections lined up in the Urdaneta South Zone. And then last night we get a few texts about DCM (District Council Meetings - that the various missionaries attend) and how that is going to mess up our scheduled times. And then Sister Foster finally responded to our text about Urdaneta 3A Sisters. They have rats in their apartment and we’ve set traps and we’ve contacted the landlord but the rats are still there. Sunday they sent us a photo of their cupboard where the rat had totally chewed it up. It’s so disgusting. So Sister Foster finally responded and said to get them out of there.


We immediately came up with a plan. We would move them from Mannix 26 - the Rat House, to Mannix 29. We use Mannix 29 for overflow and it’s where the trainers stay the night before intake. It’s a nice apartment.  and it is right across from where we used to live. We figured we could have the sisters pack up their stuff, we could load it in the pickup and we all walk to Mannix 29. The more difficult part would be to move their desks, chairs, all of the kitchen stuff, etc. But we figured we could do it.


Sister Foster said she knew a couple that would probably be able to help and they could really use the money. So - we hired them. And what an absolute blessing. Last name is Jesus - no joke! I’ll get to that story in a minute - but first lets jump back to 10AM when we left our apartment, headed for our first inspection with Urdaneta 3B Sisters. That’s the first apartment that Scott & I found. The place looked great and the sisters that live there love it.


When we finished with that inspection we headed to Urdaneta Townhomes to start cleaning and organizing Mannix 29 for when the sisters would move in a little later that day. We put loads of sheets in the washing machine, we vacuumed the bottom of all of the furniture, we took all of the kitchen stuff over to Mannix 20, we emptied closets, desks, cleaned off the back deck and back laundry room, we vacuumed the whole apartment with our handy dandy shopvac!! We worked like dogs and were sweating up a storm.


Our next inspection was at house 200, the Urdaneta Elders. That house is FULL of stuff that has been left by other Elders. Just so much stuff that belongs to no one in the mission. We’ve got a plan for that stuff! We call this apartment “The Pet Shop Boys” because it is next door to a pet shop. It’s how we remember apartments!


Then we drove to Villasis to the sisters apartment. These apartments are only 5-10 km apart but it takes all of 20-30 minutes sometimes to drive there because traffic is so slow.  Their place was super clean. We found out that their toilet doesn’t flush so they just use a bucket of water to flush it. WHAT?  That’s just not right. Scott couldn’t fix it so we’ll get our maintenance guy to put new guts in it. Another weird thing about that bathroom is that if you use the bidet, water comes out the showerhead too! So weird. So we’ll have that fixed.


We then went to Mr. Quickie to have keys made, then to the Stake Center and picked up the 3 Sisters from Urdaneta 3B and we drive to Mannix 26. They had all of their stuff packed and so we load it in the truck and drive it 50 yards to Mannix 29 where we unload it. They were so excited to move, Mannix 29 was pretty clean and EMPTY. We went back and forth a bit with the sisters, gathering items from their old place and walking them to their new place. The road is l 1/2 car widths wide and to pass you have to park on the sidewalk and the other car drives up on the other sidewalk.  So we drove forward to load up the stuff and then just backed up 50 yards to unload because you can't turn around.  And when backing up you have to weave around trikes or cars parked in the lane.  Then it was time to go to work. So the sisters left and we stayed. But our 3 workers showed up and for the next 2 hours we all lost about 50 lbs of sweat because it was so hot and humid and we were working and unloading Mannix 26 and taking apart beds, moving furniture, emptying closets that were FULL to the brim with stuff that other missionaries had just left. Sooooo much stuff. Just sooooo much stuff.


The last thing we loaded onto our truck to take to Mannix 20 for temporary storage were the mattresses. I think there were a total of 9 twin size mattresses that we moved. The older gentleman that was helping us took apart the bunk beds which is work. And he helped take apart the queen bed frames that were in Mannix 29. The guys carried all of that stuff, truckload by truckload, down to Mannix 20 for storage. By the time we left, real close to 6PM, we were absolutely drenched in sweat and exhausted. But we felt great because we love the work. We paid our helpers and headed back to Villasis Sisters to put in a lightbulb and drop off the keys we had made for them. We stopped at a vegetable stand and bought a pineapple and a few veggies. Then we headed to Manaoag -a 40 minute drive.


While we were working at Mannix, I got a call from the Manaoag Elders. They had actually tried to call me a bazillion times but I didn’t have my phone with me. But when they finally reached me around 4PM they told me that their door knob was broken. They couldn’t get it to work and it finally just broke, so they had no door knob but they needed to leave to go work. I asked them if they felt comfortable just leaving with no way to lock their place. They said they did and I told them we’d be there in an hour or so because we still had to go to Villasis. 


We worked longer than we thought we would at Mannix and we knew we had to get to a hardware store to buy an external door knob that locks with a key before the hardware stores close, which is usually 6 or 7pm. We pulled into City Hardware a little before 7, picked out a doorknob and then headed up to Manaoag. Here’s where things get interesting.


We had no intention of going north to Manaoag on that day. We were going to move the sisters, we planned an hour to do that and then we were going to head home. But here we are at City Hardware close to 7 PM, it’s dark, we’re hungry and we’ve got a 30 minute drive to get to the Elders so we can install their new doorknob. And we’re also a little bit nervous because there’s a high probability that the doorknob won’t fit the door. They don’t make doors here like they do in the states. Alot of the doors in these apartments are made one at a time. So the holes in the doors for doorknobs isn’t always uniform. 


We arrive at their apartment, we open our doorknob and slide it into the hole and it seems like it’s going to fit. We breath a sigh of relief. So now we really start putting it together with all of the pieces and we get it installed. Now we have to close the door to make sure that the doorknob will actually fit into the striker plate and keep the door closed. And . . . . it doesn’t work. The striker plate is in the wrong place. 


Elder Ostler goes to his toolbox and pulls out this sweet little chisel thing that worked beautifully at chiseling away at the hole where the striker plate goes so that we could get it in the right place.  A literal miracle is the presence of the chisel.  I had seen what looked like a screwdriver with a protecting plastic cap on the end of it before.  It was just in the tool bag.  Never paid it much attention.  Well, when we needed a small chisel to make the area for the striker plate bigger I thought I would pull out that 'new screwdriver'.  I took the protective cap off and it was exactly the tool I needed but didn't expect to have.  A brand new, sharp, small chisel!!!  God does come through.


And then my phone rings. And it is the Mapandan Sisters. And they are a bit upset. Sister AY is telling me that there is a dying cat on their back porch and they don’t know what to do. WHAT??? Did I hear that right? A dying cat? I did not sign up for figuring out what to do with dying cats at 8:45PM. I ask her a bunch of questions about what the cat is actually doing. She said it’s having a seizure and it is dying. I tell her to go to a neighbors house if she feels comfortable doing that (with her companion of course) and try to find a dad, a guy, that will come over and just take the cat away. She said that there were some members of their ward that lived close by and they would go ask them to come help. Whew - I was relieved that I didn’t have to go take care of a dying cat. 


We kept chiseling away at the door and we finally inserted the striker plate, had to chisel a bunch more to move the striker plate so the latch would catch, finally screwed it into place and the doorknob latched!!! Success. And right when we finished my phone rang again. It was Sister AY telling us that the member didn’t want to help because they were afraid of getting scratched. Seriously? Really? Ughhhhh. She asked if I wanted to see the cat and I said sure. It had been 15 minutes since her first call and I just couldn’t believe that a cat would be having a seizure for that long. She turned on her video and sure enough, a cat laying on their back porch just seizing. The Sisters were freaked out. They did not know what to do. I looked at their location on the map because I didn’t know how far away they were. I was thinking that by the time we get there the cat will be dead so why go?


I pull up their location and Google maps tells me it’s an 8 minute drive. AN 8 MINUTE DRIVE. Let me explain something here. Our apartments in the Philippines Urdaneta Mission are not 8 minutes apart. It takes FOREVER to get from apartment to apartment. They aren’t far away in distance, but when you’re only going 20 miles an hour it takes forever to get anywhere. So when I saw that it was 8 minutes away, I started to realize that something else had been going on for several hours. 


The Elders called us several times, starting at about 2PM. They called my phone 5 times but I never answered until 4PM. I told them we’d be there in an hour but so many other things happened. Our trip to Villasis, stopping at the hardware store and then we stopped at Mr Quickie again to get extra keys made for the doorknob we just bought and it was during a torrential downpour that we stopped for the keys. It just added to the craziness of the whole thing. So anyway, from the time that I talked to the Elders about their broken door knob until we actually showed up at their apartment to fix it, it had been 4 hours. It was after 8PM. The doorknob striker plate wasn’t lined up so we had to chisel for a while to make it work which took a bit of time. The missionaries are supposed to be home no later than 9pm. The sisters must have gotten home close to 8:45 cause that’s when they called me about the cat. And when she called back saying the member wouldn’t come help, we were an 8 minute drive away. The whole thing was a great big miracle.


We arrived at their house and those 4 sisters were shaken. They felt so bad for that kitty and they didn’t know what to do. We got a box out of our truck, asked them for a towel, Elder Ostler covered the seizing kitty and rolled it into the box. They were relieved but a bit horrified about what was going to happen to the kitty. I told them that the kitty was going to finally get out of its miserable body and his spirit would be free. I told them that it looked like the kitty was already dead, or mostly dead 🙂 and for some reason it’s body was still shaking. But he wasn’t in pain. I hugged them and told them to have a prayer and ask for comfort and that it would be okay. 


Then we left, found a dark road with no houses and set the box in some bushes way off the road. Did all of that just happen? We just sat there talking about the miracle of us being in Manaoag when the sisters called. They needed an adult and if we’d been in Urdaneta it would have taken us forever to get to them. We saw the hand of our Heavenly Father in our movements that night. We were at the Elders apartment, 8 minutes away, at the exact right time we needed to be there in order to help the Mapandan sisters. They were soooooo grateful and thankful. I could tell that they were a little spooked and would probably have preferred it if we could stay for just a bit and talk to them. But we wanted to get kitty away from them so we didn’t stay long. 


What a day. I say that alot. But WOW - what a day. We love our mission. 



The next day during inspections we were in a sister's apartment and it started to rain.  Like pour, like you are under a shower.  Within minutes the streets are running rivers.  We had parked just 30 yards down the street.  I had to get something out of the truck.  I borrowed the sister's umbrella and walked in 3-4 inches of water at times to get to the truck.  Soakers.  I also got Delene's umbrella.  I also backed up the truck so it was right outside the gate and left it in the middle of the road - when it pours like that the roads empty because over half of the traffic would be motorcycles or trikes and they take refuge under bridges, under trees etc. I get back to the apartment and I'm soaked.  We leave under Delene's umbrella and it helps a bit but inevitably we get more wet.  As Delene is sitting in the passenger seat with the door open - she is trying to close the umbrella which she is holding outside the truck.  It is a nice umbrella and the closing mechanism is a bit tight and with the rain pouring down she can't quite grip it well - too slick.  So she is getting soaked trying to close the umbrella.  We are like that for about 2-3 mintues - in the truck, door open on her side, umbrella sticking out, rain coming in on her right side, getting frustrated that the umbrella won't close.  So finally, I run around to the passenger side, grab the umbrella and get in my side and use a bit more muscle and get it closed.  We are now frustrated, soaked clear thru and its too crazy.  Luckily by the time we get to our next house to inspect the rain is now lighter so that's good.  But we are soaked.  So whether it's rain or our own sweat we are always soaked here.


On Friday, we went to our first Necrological Service for a sister in our ward that died. Her name was V. We didn’t know her but we knew members of her family in our ward. We show up and there are 2 big canopy/tents set up in the road in front of her house and there are tables and chairs and people are playing card games and chatting. Shortly after we arrived the service started.

Some things you WILL NOT see in the states.



1) Walking down the street with your Carabao

2) A Trike Load of bananas

3) A guy sleeping in a hammock in his trike

4) Piles and piles of ginger at the market

5) Plumbing under a kitchen sink in a junior missionary apartment
