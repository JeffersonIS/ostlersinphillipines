Dear Family & Friends:

Shout out to Harrison for the wonderful email and photos. When we saw the picture of the 'mud garden' Elder Ostler said 'Best use of a garden EVER!' And thanks for the email Mom - I hope you are dad are doing well and enjoying my kids when they come to visit. I know they all love being on the farm.

Another week unlike any week we've had so far. They are all that way, no two days are ever the same. And that is one reason we absolutely love this mission experience.

But sometimes it feels like we’re just moving stuff around. We take apart bunkbeds, we put them back together. We move boxes of kitchen stuff from an apartment to our house to storage at Mannix 35. We haul dead broken fans, tons of them, we haul boxes and boxes of brand new fans that we have become very proficient in putting together. We move wardrobes, washing machines, mattresses, desks and chairs. So yeah, sometimes it just seems like we move stuff around, but then . . . . we have Zone Conference and we come face to face with 73 junior missionaries (half of our missionaries, the other half meets on a different day) that absolutely love us and worship the ground we walk on and thank us profusely for getting their toilet to work or making their gate stop squeaking or replacing their fan that was missing it’s cage and it would cut their arm if they got too close to it, or giving them a ride to their appointment with their investigators or contacting their landlord because they have water pouring in from their ceiling. They thank us for making their apartments livable and getting all of the leaky plumbing fixed. They thank us for bringing them a new 2 burner cooktop so that all 4 of them in the apartment can cook on more than one burner, or actually cook on the burner without having to use a lighter to light them. They are over the top excited when they find out that 'pancake turner' is on the list of approved items for junior missionary housing and we can bring one to them!


One of the best reactions that I can remember from all of our inspections was when Elder Ostler got up on a chair in a shower and replaced a burnt out light bulb. One of the elders that lived in that apartment was standing over by me and we were talking about who knows what and he saw the light go on in the shower and he jumped and hollered and clapped and skipped into the bathroom to get a closer look at the new lightbulb. He was over the top happy . . . with a new light bulb. 


We love these 140+ missionaries that are serving in our mission. We were honored this week when President Foster asked us to teach at zone conference. Our topic was page 10 of Preach My Gospel, The Gospel of Jesus Christ Blesses All of God’s Children. We taught on the 6 paragraphs under that heading. It was a beautiful experience.


As we’ve inspected the apartments we’ve noticed alot of JUNK. So much stuff that belongs to no one. It’s just been left by missionaries that didn’t take their stuff with them or things that are broken or things no one uses anymore. We’ve been talking to Sister Foster about the mountains of junk in some of these apartments and she decided to do a mission wide project to Collect The Junk - The CTJ Movement. We had a meeting on zoom last week where Elder Ostler and I made a kahoot about housing and apartments and about keeping them clean and it was pretty fun. During the meeting we introduced the movement. We had been handing out big black trash bags to every apartment for a few weeks as we went on inspections. And at the meeting we told them it was time to fill those bags with all of the junk in their apartment. Everything that didn’t belong to them or their companion needed to go. If the entire mission could collect 50 bags of JUNK, every missionary would get a candybar at Zone Conference. If the mission collected at least 75 bags, every companionship would get a bag of M&M’s. The final count was announced at zone conference. They had to take a picture of them by all of their bags of junk in order for it to count. And the final number of bags of JUNK collected was . . . . 131. Can you believe it???? We can believe it because we see it everytime we go into an apartment. It will be so nice to have those piles gone. And it will be so much easier for the missionaries to keep their apartments clean because the JUNK is gone. And the interesting part is that their JUNK is someone else's treasure.  You can leave a bag of used items, items not even yard sale worthy in the states and it will be gone, picked over and disappear overnight.  We came back to our old apartment where we dump a lot of our garbage and put 2 bags of JUNK from a sister's apartment on the ground next to the garbage cans.  No sooner had they hit the ground than 2 people started going through the stuff.  Old shoes, books, shirts, mismatched pots and pans, anything you can imagine missionaries discard---well these items were gone in 10 minutes.  I give all the old pillows (stained yellow from sweat and drool) to an old guy that we used to live by and you would think it was Christmas.


The Barlows are leaving in a couple of weeks so President and Sister Foster had a BBQ at their home this week. It was just us, The Fosters and The Barlows. And the food was out of this world fantastic. Sister Foster made a potato salad that I just don’t have words for. And President Foster just got a grill and the burgers tasted just like home. Sooooo yummy. It was a super fun time to just be with the adults, talking about our families and enjoying yummy food together. We are going to miss the Barlows. We don’t spend hardly any time with them, but just knowing that there is another couple that is working and supporting the mission and is just there, it’s comforting knowing they are here. They have 2 kids serving missions right now too. And that’s pretty cool to be serving a mission at the same time that your kids are serving.



We've had some great experiences this week.  As you may recall we had to move the Sisters out of their burning oven apartment.  We were very concerned about breaking the lease since we just signed a year lease.  We first signed another lease with the apartment we found for them, paid 3 months rent for the apartment and then we went to break the lease with the burning oven apartment.  So many miracles with this deal.  First - to backtrack.  When we signed the original lease on July 1 we had proof that the money for the contract had left our bank.  A screenshot of the church web account showing money was paid to their bank.  Well, the owner could not see the money on his end, the email that the church usually sends was not there but we told him it was probably a bank glitch and his bank had not yet processed the payment.  We had already paid 1/3 months rent as a deposit so he let us move in.  Then a few days later the church account showed the payment as pending.  From paid to pending.  Super strange.  Then we decided to move the sisters out because it was unliveable.  So we told our finance people to tell the Manilla church headquarters to stop payment.  And then we took 10,000 pesos to fulfill the rent payment for the rest of July and told the owner we were giving our 30 day notice and July was paid in full.  So God intervened at the bank level.  The money was paid, then God reversed it and it showed as pending, then Manilla stopped all payment and no more money went to the owner.  This is not how banking works.  Such a miracle.  And to back up again in the story - Delene and I were very concerned about the owner's reaction to when we presented our termination letter and paid the remaining rent.  I was a bit tied up in knots thinking about it.  But the morning we did this I woke up very calm, scriptures running through my head, just a peaceful feeling like it was God's will.  I thought of the scripture in Kings where the prophet told his servant that there are more with us than against us.  And so when we went into the owner's office to give our 10K and termination letter there was a surreal peace and calm about the whole situation.  The owner said she understood, offered us to live in one of their other units, didn't balk at the fact or realize that our 30 day notice would end mid-August instead of the end of July (which is all we were paying for).  It kind of felt like the scene from Star Wars where Luke was using a Jedi mind trick to get his way.  I know it was the Spirit that preceded us and accompanied us and calmed any adversarial reaction the owner might have had.  God orchestrated this whole thing.



Then we spent until 9pm moving the sisters out. 4 truck loads of stuff, fridge, suitcases, sisters, mattresses, beds, you name it. We moved it all. And we were exhausted in the end. We even had to go back to their old apartment from a week ago to get a few more things for them. We have had to go back a few times to give them new padlocks on their gates, bring them working fans, put together a shelving unit with a bazillion nuts and bolts for their pantry - and they love their new place. It is 100 yards from the beach and you can definitely feel the beach breeze which is way cooler than their burning oven apartment.



And in case you were wondering, we had our 2nd piano class on Saturday. This was a day that it dumped rain. And we got quite a few inquiries from ward members asking if class was cancelled. Evidently the Filipinos don't get out much when it rains. So cancelling things due to heavy rains is a real thing. But we held class and had 20+ people show up. So that's good



Speaking of rain. Today we got caught in a deluge. Just a downpour. Even with umbrellas we were just soaked. We had to walk from an apartment thru a back alley/road/driveway which ended up being a small river with puddles the whole way. Can you say 'soakers'? And then driving to our next place. Rivers of water just pouring down streets, thru homes into the rice paddies behind the homes. This isn't Kansas for sure. And we noticed that no kids were in school. Evidently school was cancelled due to the rain and threat of typhoon type rains. The Elders told us this happens quite regularly.



Well, we love all of you. We love how you take care of each other. We sure missed the mini-reunion at TBH. And we love being here.



Elder and Sister Ostler

Mom and Dad

Granny and Grandpa
